## 2026-11-25 — Reference experiments

**Question.** What gravitational and electromagnetic effects does established physics predict for the candidate geometries?

**GRV-0001.** For a radius of 0.25 m, 50 turns per ring and 2 A peak currents in quadrature, the simulated central field had a magnitude of 0.35967 mT at each sampled phase. The field vector matched the analytical prediction and the center satisfied the gravity symmetry check. The maximum modeled Newtonian acceleration over the sampled locations was 1.35e-11 m/s². At the axial sample at time zero, magnetic force was about 9.97e-16 N and the sample's gravitational force from coil mass was about 1.32e-16 N.

The maximum local magnetic energy estimate was 5.15e-11 J in the specified cube, equivalent to about 5.73e-28 kg by E/c². The estimate has limited spatial coverage. The model uses source rest masses in the Newtonian calculation. The benchmark stores this result as a baseline for conventional effects.

**GRV-0002.** A uniform 1,000 kg disk of radius 2 m had a peak modeled acceleration of 3.17e-8 m/s² across heights from 0.1 to 2.1 m. For the same geometry, the following masses produce the target accelerations at 1 m.

| Target | Mass (kg) |
| --- | ---: |
| 0.01g | 5.32e9 |
| Lunar | 8.78e10 |
| Martian | 2.01e11 |
| Earth | 5.32e11 |

The model predicts a vertical acceleration gradient, so the target at 1 m does not hold uniformly across a standing person's height. The Earth target mass has a rest-energy equivalent of 4.78e28 J.

**Negative result.** In these established baseline models, the coil drive changes magnetic observables while the gravitational field follows the fixed distribution of coil rest mass. The numerical result bounds this specific modeled configuration and leaves any additional gravity hypothesis to a separate test.

**Open questions.** How sensitive are the field gradients to the true winding geometry and sample anisotropy? How much does disk acceleration vary across off-axis points or alternative radial mass profiles? These questions require independent models and validation data.
